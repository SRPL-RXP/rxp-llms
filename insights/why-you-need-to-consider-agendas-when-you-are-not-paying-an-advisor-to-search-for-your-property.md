The article argues that healthcare occupiers searching for commercial space should pay close attention to whose interests an "advisor" actually represents when no fee is being paid for the search. In commercial real estate, an agent who is not appointed by the tenant is appointed by the landlord, by a developer with a building to fill, or by a referral partner with a financial interest in a particular outcome. The agendas are not hidden in the sense of being secret. They're hidden in the sense of being structurally invisible if you don't ask.

The piece sets out the distinction between an agent acting for one side under a clear appointment and an agent who appears helpful but is monetised through other relationships, and offers a framework for healthcare occupiers to assess whose interests are actually being served in any given search.
